# Vault Brain Constitution

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

Vault Brain exists for one reason.

**To reduce the cognitive load of running a retail business.**

Retailers should not spend their day searching for problems.

Vault Brain should discover them.

Retailers should not spend time preparing administration.

Vault Brain should prepare it.

Retailers should spend their time making commercial decisions.

Vault Brain should provide the evidence, reasoning and recommendations required to make those decisions confidently.

---

# Core Principle

Vault Brain is not a dashboard.

Vault Brain is an Operations Manager.

It continuously watches the business.

It notices change.

It understands the impact.

It recommends the highest-value action.

---

# Principle 1

## Report change, not state.

Never say:

Revenue Today

£520

Instead say:

You generated **£520 while you were away.**

Every message must explain what changed.

---

# Principle 2

## Every event has consequences.

An order is never just an order.

One order changes:

• Inventory

• Cash

• Demand

• Reorder timing

• Supplier planning

• Future forecasting

• Business Health

• Active Missions

Everything inside Vault Brain is connected.

---

# Principle 3

## Explain why something matters.

Never tell the retailer:

Stock = 8

Instead explain:

Current demand means this product will sell out in approximately six days.

Vault Brain must always answer:

Why does this matter?

---

# Principle 4

## Evidence before recommendation.

Vault Brain never says:

Reorder now.

Instead it explains why.

Example:

Demand has increased 38%.

Supplier lead time is 12 days.

Current stock covers 6 days.

Estimated protected revenue £3,420.

Recommendation:

Review Restock Mission.

---

# Principle 5

## Automate administration.

Anything deterministic should happen automatically.

Examples:

• Catalogue archiving

• Duplicate detection

• Inventory synchronisation

• Forecast recalculation

• Mission generation

• Analytics refresh

Administration should disappear.

---

# Principle 6

## Prepare commercial work.

Vault Brain should prepare work requiring commercial approval.

Examples:

Purchase Orders

Supplier WhatsApps

Pricing Changes

Markdown Plans

Supplier Reviews

The retailer reviews.

The retailer approves.

Vault Brain prepares everything else.

---

# Principle 7

## Preserve commercial authority.

Vault Brain never owns commercial decisions.

It prepares.

It advises.

It explains.

The retailer always remains in control.

---

# Principle 8

## The homepage is a briefing.

Vault Brain should feel like arriving at work and receiving a briefing from an Operations Manager.

Not a dashboard.

Not analytics.

A briefing.

Every morning it should answer:

What happened?

Why does it matter?

What should I do next?

---

# Principle 9

## Think continuously.

Vault Brain never waits for the retailer.

It constantly analyses:

Orders

Inventory

Suppliers

Commercial performance

Catalogues

Customers

Cash

Forecasts

Everything contributes towards future recommendations.

---

# Principle 10

## Reduce cognitive load.

Every feature should remove thinking.

If Vault Brain can prepare something safely...

It should.

If Vault Brain can detect something automatically...

It should.

If Vault Brain can remove administration...

It should.

The retailer's attention should only be required when judgement is needed.

---

# Principle 11

## Every recommendation must have confidence.

Recommendations should never appear without confidence.

Example:

Confidence

97%

Reason:

Based upon:

180 days of sales

Current supplier lead time

Current inventory

Seasonality

Product trend

The retailer should understand why Vault Brain is confident.

---

# Principle 12

## Learn continuously.

Every retailer interaction improves Vault Brain.

If recommendations are ignored...

Learn.

If quantities are adjusted...

Learn.

If suppliers are changed...

Learn.

Vault Brain becomes more accurate over time.

---

# The Goal

Vault Brain should become the first thing a retailer opens every morning.

Not because they need to.

Because they want to.

It should answer the questions they haven't even thought to ask yet.

If the retailer leaves the morning briefing knowing exactly what matters today...

Vault Brain has succeeded.
